Revision Note — What This Document Does and Does Not Change
This is a second edition of the Blueprint-First AI Software Engineering architecture. It is not a rewrite. Every stage, engine, and named system from the original document is preserved: the AI Executive Orchestrator, the Application Intelligence System, the Product DNA Library, the Application Genome Engine, the Application Knowledge Graph, the Product/Page/Workflow Discovery Engines, Complete Product Discovery, the Page Completeness Engine, the Feature Expansion Engine, the Risk & Assumption Register, Recursive Self-Discovery, the Multi-Perspective Reasoning Council, the Requirements Traceability Engine, Design-to-Code Traceability, the Product Coverage Engine, Blueprint Completeness Certification, Blueprint Confidence, the AI Design Studio, the Digital Twin, the Live Verification Engine, the Master Verification Engine, Blueprint Approval, the AI Build Studio, AI Acceptance Testing, AI Operations & Observability, the Permanent Engineering Organization, the AI Engineering Guardian, the AI Self-Healing Engineering System, the Living Blueprint, Continuous Product Evolution, Engineering Memory, Safe Change Intelligence, the Continuous Learning Engine, and the AI Router.
None of these are replaced. All of them are strengthened, and — critically — connected to two things the original architecture named but did not fully mechanize:
A real organizational structure for Discovery. The original Stage 0 described what discovery must find. This edition describes who finds it, who checks it, who is allowed to declare it complete, and what happens when they disagree — a Discovery Department with named worker roles, specialist verifiers, an independent Discovery Boss, and a red team, operating through a fixed worker → self-verification → specialist verification → boss review → independent audit → certification pipeline.
A verification standard that counts alone cannot satisfy. The original architecture asserted that nothing advances without evidence. This edition defines exactly what “evidence” means: an eleven-dimension check (count, coverage, identity, correctness, quality, traceability, dependency integrity, duplication, conflicts, consistency, evidence-of-work) that every certification in the platform — not only Discovery’s — must pass before anything is marked complete.
Everything added here follows the same architectural grain as the original: it extends existing engines rather than duplicating them, and it applies one governance pattern consistently rather than inventing a different one per stage. Where a new mechanism is introduced, it is introduced once, named clearly, and then reused everywhere the same problem recurs — inside Discovery, inside Design, inside Build, inside Test, and inside the Permanent Engineering Organization that owns an application for life.

PART I — THE PHILOSOPHY (RESTATED AND EXTENDED)
The original philosophy stands unchanged: software is not generated, it is engineered — understood first, architected second, validated third, and only then built. The platform must never behave like an AI code generator with a chat window attached. It must behave like an elite engineering organization that refuses to skip a step regardless of how confident any individual AI seems.
This edition adds three governing principles that follow directly from that philosophy and apply to every stage described below, not only to Discovery.
Production and Judgment are different jobs, performed by different reasoning. A system that produces work and then grades its own work has not been verified — it has been narrated. Everywhere this architecture uses the word “verified,” it means a second, independent line of reasoning examined the artifact against an independently-derived expectation, not that the original producer explained itself convincingly. This is the reason every department in this document follows the same worker → verifier → boss → audit → certification structure: separation of production from judgment is not a discovery-specific idea, it is the platform’s operating principle.
A count is a symptom, not proof. “We produced 200 pages” and “we were supposed to produce 200 pages” together prove nothing — the platform could have produced 200 wrong pages, 200 duplicate pages, or 199 correct pages plus one invented one. Every completeness claim anywhere in the platform must be evaluated on the eleven-dimension standard defined in this edition (§0.16), not on whether a target number was hit.
Nothing is “done” because a worker said so. Completion is a certified state, reached only by passing through the stage’s verification chain and clearing every dimension of the completeness standard. The original manifesto implied this; this edition makes it a formal state machine (§0.17) that every artifact in the Application Knowledge Graph — not only pages — is subject to, from first discovery through permanent production operation.

PART II — THE STRENGTHENED TARGET ARCHITECTURE
STAGE 0 OF 5 — TARGET END-STATE ARCHITECTURE
Stage 0: AI Intelligence & Product Discovery Layer — Now a Full Discovery Organization
The original architecture correctly identified Discovery as the layer everything else depends on: if Discovery misses something, Design never designs it, Build never implements it, Testing never tests it, and Deployment never contains it. This edition takes that dependency seriously and restructures Stage 0 from a set of engines into a real organizational department — with workers, specialist verifiers, a boss with independent judgment, an adversarial red team, and an auditor — because a single AI agent asking itself “did I miss anything?” cannot supply the independence the original philosophy demands.
Discovery’s mandate, stated formally, is:
Construct the most complete, internally consistent, traceable, evidence-backed representation of the product that can reasonably be derived before design and implementation begin — answering not only “what did the user ask for,” but “what does this category of product actually require,” judged from the vantage point of a senior product manager, architect, UX designer, backend engineer, QA lead, security lead, DevOps lead, and the enterprise customers and end users the product must ultimately satisfy.
0.1 Governance of the Discovery Organization
The AI Executive Orchestrator (preserved, unchanged in authority) continues to govern the whole platform. Reporting into it, Stage 0 now has its own internal executive: the Chief Discovery Officer (Discovery Boss) — the independent judgment described in Part IV below, and the role that the original document gestured at but did not name. The Discovery Boss does not perform discovery work; it is structurally prohibited from doing so, for the same reason a financial auditor does not also keep the books. Its sole function is independent reconstruction and comparison, described fully in §0.15.
Beneath the Discovery Boss sit the department’s three functional layers:
The Foundational Intelligence Layer — the platform’s permanent, cross-project knowledge (Application Intelligence System, Product DNA Library, Application Genome Engine, Application Knowledge Graph). Preserved from the original architecture; extended in this edition only with the artifact identification and metadata standards needed to make everything downstream traceable (§0.12, §0.13).
The Discovery Worker Corps — the specialized roles that actually perform each discovery discipline (§0.3).
The Verification Chain — self-verification, specialist verifiers, the Discovery Boss, the Discovery Red Team, and the Discovery Auditor (§0.4).
0.2 The Foundational Intelligence Layer (Preserved, Extended)
The Application Intelligence System, Product DNA Library, Application Genome Engine, and Application Knowledge Graph operate exactly as originally specified: a permanent, cross-project knowledge layer modeling software, product, UX, business, system, enterprise, domain, workflow, security, and data architecture; a continually evolving library of what a complete product looks like per category; a genome-matching engine that infers what the user did not think to ask for; and a single connected graph tying every requirement, feature, workflow, page, component, rule, API, database entity, permission, notification, integration, test, and deployment artifact together bidirectionally.
This edition adds one structural requirement to the Knowledge Graph: every node must be independently retrievable by a stable artifact ID (§0.12) and must carry the metadata schema defined in §0.13. A discovery that cannot be cited by ID, traced to the pass that found it, and scored for confidence is not yet a discovery the platform can certify — it is a note. This is what allows the Requirements Traceability Engine and Design-to-Code Traceability, both preserved unchanged in purpose, to actually function at the scale of hundreds or thousands of artifacts rather than only in principle.
0.3 The Discovery Worker Corps — Full Individual Specifications
Discovery is decomposed into 36 specialized roles across five clusters. Every role below is specified individually across twelve fields: responsibility, inputs, outputs/artifacts, evidence required, dependencies, verification, failure conditions, rejection/rework behavior, interaction with the Discovery Boss, interaction with the Red Team/Auditor, contribution to recursive discovery, and contribution to the Master Product Inventory. No two roles perform the same function: Cluster A decides what the product is, Cluster B decides what it is made of, Cluster C decides how that structure behaves, Cluster D decides what it must satisfy beyond stated functionality, and Cluster E exists solely to attack the other four clusters’ confidence.
Cluster A — Understanding Workers
Purpose: build the platform’s model of what the product is, for whom, and why, before any structural discovery begins.
DW-A1. Product Understanding Worker
Responsibility: Produce the first, most upstream artifact in the Knowledge Graph: a structured statement of what the user is asking to build, in the user’s own terms, before any inference is layered on top of it.
Inputs: The user’s vision statement, any supplied documents, prior conversation history, and — when extending an existing application — the current Living Blueprint (§5.3).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Product Understanding Brief, tagged PROJECT-001: stated goals, stated target users, stated constraints, stated non-goals.
Evidence Required: Every stated goal, user, and constraint must be a direct paraphrase traceable to a specific sentence or document passage in the inputs — not an inference.
Dependencies: None upstream; this is the department’s entry point.
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: Vision too sparse to reach minimum confidence; brief contradicts itself; a stated goal cannot be traced to any input.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Generates Discovery Questions for the user and marks the brief LOW-CONFIDENCE rather than fabricating detail; never resubmitted until the ambiguity is resolved or explicitly accepted as a logged assumption.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: The Boss’s independent reconstruction (§0.15) begins from this brief and the Domain Profile only — it does not read any downstream worker’s output, so an error here propagates directly into the Boss’s own expectation and is caught the next time the Boss re-derives it from source.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Not directly challenged by the Red Team (§0.10), which operates on structural/behavioral/non-functional inventories; challenged instead by the Contradiction Analysis Worker if later artifacts imply a goal this brief did not state.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Any Discovery Question this worker raises can trigger re-entry into Pass 1 once the user responds, before Pass 2 proceeds.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: Seeds PROJECT-001, the root node every other artifact in the Master Product Inventory ultimately traces back to.
DW-A2. Domain Understanding Worker
Responsibility: Determine the industry/domain the product belongs to and pull the relevant conventions, vocabulary, and regulatory context from the Product DNA Library.
Inputs: Product Understanding Brief (PROJECT-001); Product DNA Library domain models.
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Domain Profile: named domain, applicable regulatory frameworks, domain vocabulary, and known domain-specific workflow conventions.
Evidence Required: Each domain claim cites the Product DNA Library entry it was drawn from; each regulatory flag cites the specific requirement category it implies.
Dependencies: Product Understanding Brief (DW-A1).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: Domain misclassified; a materially applicable regulatory framework omitted; vocabulary conflicts with the user’s own stated terms without being flagged.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Returned to DW-A2 with the specific misclassification identified; a misclassified domain forces re-run of Pass 3 and Pass 4 downstream.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss independently classifies the domain from the same brief before comparing; a domain mismatch is treated as a Priority-1 delta because every downstream genome inference depends on it.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team challenges under-scoped regulatory findings specifically (Part 10 category: compliance) once the full inventory exists.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A later-discovered feature (e.g., payments) can trigger DW-A2 to re-check for a regulatory framework not initially flagged (e.g., PCI-DSS).
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: Attaches the Domain Profile to PROJECT-001; this profile is a required citation for every Compliance Discovery Worker (DW-D6) finding.
DW-A3. Industry Pattern Research Worker
Responsibility: Survey mature, real-world products in the same category to build the raw material the Industry Comparison Engine (§0.9) evaluates for completeness gaps.
Inputs: Category Classification (DW-A5 output); Domain Profile (DW-A2 output).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Industry Pattern Report: a catalogue of common modules, workflows, administrative surfaces, and conventions observed in mature products of this category, each tagged with its source pattern.
Evidence Required: Each pattern entry names the class of product it is drawn from and the specific capability observed; patterns are never asserted without a named category-level precedent.
Dependencies: Category Classification (DW-A5).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: Report reflects only one sub-style of the category (e.g., only enterprise patterns, ignoring SMB conventions) without disclosing that scope limitation; patterns listed with no clear capability description.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Industry Comparison Engine bounces the report back if a pattern is too vague to evaluate as Required/Not-Required/Uncertain.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Not directly reconstructed by the Discovery Boss; the Boss instead checks that the Industry Comparison Engine’s Add/Exclude/Question resolutions (§0.9) were actually applied to the Master Product Inventory.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team may independently propose patterns DW-A3 missed; both feed the same Industry Comparison Engine resolution queue.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A pattern resolved ‘Required’ can trigger new Page, Feature, or Workflow Discovery passes exactly like a Red Team or Negative-Space finding.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: Contributes evidence citations to any Master Product Inventory artifact whose origin is industry-comparison rather than user statement or genome inference.
DW-A4. Business Model Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Determine how the product earns, what it commercially depends on, and what commercial risk it carries.
Inputs: Product Understanding Brief (DW-A1); Domain Profile (DW-A2).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Business Model Brief: revenue mechanism(s), commercial dependencies (e.g., a payment processor, a marketplace of suppliers), and commercial risk flags.
Evidence Required: Each revenue mechanism and dependency must be either stated by the user or drawn from a named Product DNA Library pattern for this category — never invented.
Dependencies: Product Understanding Brief (DW-A1), Domain Profile (DW-A2).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: Revenue mechanism assumed without evidence; a commercial dependency (e.g., subscription billing) identified but not flagged to the Risk & Assumption Register.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Unsupported revenue or dependency claims are stripped and reopened as Discovery Questions.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss checks specifically that every commercial dependency named here has a corresponding entry in the Risk & Assumption Register (§0.20) before certifying Pass 1 complete.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team checks for uninvestigated commercial edge cases this brief implies (e.g., failed subscription renewal) once Feature Discovery (DW-B3) has run.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A newly discovered commercial dependency can trigger re-entry into Feature Discovery for the supporting capability (e.g., billing failure handling).
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: Attaches business-model constraints to PROJECT-001; consumed directly by Module Discovery (DW-B2) and Feature Discovery (DW-B3).
DW-A5. Product Category Analysis Worker
Responsibility: Classify the product against the Application Genome Engine’s category taxonomy so the correct genome and DNA-library baseline is applied.
Inputs: Product Understanding Brief (DW-A1); Application Genome Engine taxonomy.
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Category Classification: primary category and, where the product spans more than one, secondary categories with a stated boundary between them.
Evidence Required: Classification must cite the specific taxonomy definition matched, not merely a category label.
Dependencies: Product Understanding Brief (DW-A1).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: Product force-fit into a single category when it genuinely spans two (e.g., ‘CRM’ when the product is CRM + billing); category chosen without citing the taxonomy definition.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Returned for re-classification; every downstream Application Genome Analysis (Pass 4) is blocked until this passes self-verification.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss independently classifies the category from the Product Understanding Brief before seeing this worker’s classification, then diffs — the same reconstruction discipline as §0.15, applied at the category level first because every subsequent genome inference inherits from it.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team checks whether a secondary category was missed (e.g., a marketplace that is also, structurally, a CRM for sellers).
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A Red Team or Boss-identified secondary category re-triggers Pass 4 (Application Genome Analysis) for the added category.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: Determines which Product DNA Library baseline every Structural (Cluster B) worker inherits.
Cluster B — Structural Discovery Workers
Purpose: enumerate what the product is made of.
DW-B1. User/Role Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Identify every meaningful user role the product must support and what each role must be able to accomplish.
Inputs: Product Understanding Brief (DW-A1), Category Classification (DW-A5), Application Genome output (Pass 4).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Role Inventory, tagged ROLE-000n: role name, purpose, and the capability boundary of the role.
Evidence Required: Each role cites either a direct user statement or a genome-inferred role typical of this category (e.g., ‘Admin’ inferred for any multi-tenant SaaS genome).
Dependencies: Category Classification (DW-A5); Application Genome Engine output.
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: An operationally necessary role omitted (e.g., no ‘Support Agent’ role in a product with a support workflow); two roles defined with overlapping, unresolved capability boundaries.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Returned with the specific missing or overlapping role identified by would-be ROLE- ID; re-verified against the updated Workflow Inventory (DW-B4) once workflows are discovered, since roles and workflows co-determine each other.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss independently derives the expected role set from the genome and domain before comparing against the Role Inventory by ID, exactly as specified in §0.15.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team specifically probes for administrative and operational roles (e.g., ‘Auditor’, ‘Billing Admin’) that functional-only thinking tends to omit.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Every new Feature or Workflow discovered later in the sequence is checked against the Role Inventory for a role it implies but that is not yet present (Pass 21 re-entry).
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: ROLE-000n entries are required parents for every Permission Discovery (DW-C6) finding.
DW-B2. Module Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover the product’s modules — the top-level functional groupings the rest of Structural Discovery organizes beneath.
Inputs: Business Model Brief (DW-A4), Role Inventory (DW-B1), Application Genome output.
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Module Inventory, tagged MODULE-000n.
Evidence Required: Each module cites either a direct user statement, a genome inference, or an Industry Comparison resolution (§0.9).
Dependencies: Role Inventory (DW-B1); Application Genome Engine output.
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A module genome-implied by the category is missing (e.g., no ‘Settings’ module); two modules defined with unclear ownership of a shared capability.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Missing module reopens Pass 7 for the specific module only, not the full inventory.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss reconstructs the expected module list independently from the genome before diffing by MODULE- ID.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team challenges module boundaries specifically for administrative and operational modules (Settings, Audit, Billing) that get proposed late or not at all.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A module accepted from Negative-Space Discovery (§0.11) re-enters Pass 7/8 for its own Feature Discovery sweep.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: MODULE-000n is the direct parent of every FEATURE-000n artifact.
DW-B3. Feature Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover the features within every module.
Inputs: Module Inventory (DW-B2); Application Genome output; Business Model Brief (DW-A4).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Feature Inventory, tagged FEATURE-000n, each with a parent: MODULE-000n reference.
Evidence Required: Each feature cites its source: user statement, genome inference, or industry comparison resolution.
Dependencies: Module Inventory (DW-B2).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A feature exists with no parent module; a feature genome-implied by its module is missing (e.g., a Customers module with no ‘merge duplicate customers’ feature).
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Orphan features are either reparented or rejected; missing genome-implied features reopen Pass 8 for that module only.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss reconstructs the expected feature list per module independently before diffing by ID — this is the level at which the worked example in §1.4 below operates.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team’s primary target: proposes features the worker corps is structurally likely to miss (recovery, bulk operations, audit, export) per module.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Feature Family Expansion (§0.7, Pass 11) re-enters this worker’s output to generate each feature’s full supporting ecosystem.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: FEATURE-000n is the direct parent of every PAGE-000n and WORKFLOW-000n that implements it.
DW-B4. Workflow Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover all major and supporting workflows that connect features into a working product.
Inputs: Feature Inventory (DW-B3); Role Inventory (DW-B1).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Workflow Inventory, tagged WORKFLOW-000n, each naming its participating roles and the features it connects.
Evidence Required: Each workflow cites the features and roles it connects by ID; a workflow with only one participating role is flagged for review (most cross-cutting workflows involve more than one).
Dependencies: Feature Inventory (DW-B3), Role Inventory (DW-B1).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A feature exists with no workflow that exercises it (an ‘orphan feature’ per Pass 20); a workflow references a role not present in the Role Inventory.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Orphan features are routed back to DW-B4 to either produce the missing workflow or justify why none is needed (e.g., a pure configuration feature).
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss independently derives the expected workflow set from the Feature and Role Inventories before diffing by WORKFLOW- ID.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team specifically hunts for approval chains, escalation paths, and multi-role handoffs that single-role thinking omits.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Every workflow discovered here becomes a required input to Edge-Case Discovery (DW-D1) and Recursive Page Expansion Layer 3 (Interactions).
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: WORKFLOW-000n entries connect ROLE-, FEATURE-, and PAGE- nodes into the Application Knowledge Graph’s process layer.
DW-B5. Page Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover every page, sub-page, dashboard, settings surface, admin surface, error experience, and other supporting experience the product requires.
Inputs: Feature Inventory (DW-B3); Workflow Inventory (DW-B4).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Page Inventory, tagged PAGE-00nn, each with parent: FEATURE-000n.
Evidence Required: Each page cites the feature or workflow step it exists to serve.
Dependencies: Feature Inventory (DW-B3), Workflow Inventory (DW-B4).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A feature has no page surfacing it and no documented reason why (e.g., API-only feature); a page exists that serves no feature or workflow (orphan, Pass 20).
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Missing pages reopen Pass 9 for the specific feature; orphan pages are reparented or rejected.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss independently reconstructs the expected page count and identity per feature/module before diffing — the primary artifact in the worked example (§1.4): 200 vs. 203 pages, by ID.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team’s highest-value target — administrative, error, and edge-state pages (bulk-action confirmation, session-expired, empty-search-results) are the most commonly under-discovered page class.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Every certified page immediately enters Pass 10, Recursive Page Expansion (§0.6).
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: PAGE-00nn is the anchor artifact for the entire Recursive Page Expansion layer stack (Sections/Contents/Actions/States/Validations).
DW-B6. Modal/Dialog Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover every modal, dialog, and wizard the product requires — surfaces that exist inside a page’s flow rather than as independently navigable pages.
Inputs: Page Inventory (DW-B5); Interaction Discovery Worker (DW-C2) preliminary findings.
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Modal/Dialog Inventory, tagged PAGE-00nn-MODAL-0n, parented to the page that triggers them.
Evidence Required: Each modal cites the specific interaction (e.g., ‘Delete Customer’ action) that triggers it.
Dependencies: Page Inventory (DW-B5).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A destructive action (delete, bulk-cancel) has no confirmation modal; a multi-step process is missing its wizard decomposition.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Missing confirmation modals for destructive actions are treated as a Security/Edge-Case cross-finding and routed jointly to DW-B6 and DW-D1.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss checks specifically that every destructive Interaction (Layer 3) artifact has a corresponding confirmation modal before certifying the page complete.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team specifically probes for missing multi-step wizard decomposition on complex creation flows (e.g., ‘Create Project’ collapsed into one form when it should be a wizard).
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A newly discovered modal is itself subject to Recursive Page Expansion Layers 2–7 (content, interactions, states, validation, rules, permissions) at a reduced but non-zero scope.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: PAGE-00nn-MODAL-0n nodes are children of their triggering page and parents of their own Content/Action/State artifacts.
DW-B7. Dashboard Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover every dashboard and its reporting surfaces.
Inputs: Module Inventory (DW-B2); Role Inventory (DW-B1).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Dashboard Inventory, tagged PAGE-00nn (dashboards are pages with a specialized content profile) plus REPORT-000n children.
Evidence Required: Each dashboard cites the role(s) it serves and the decisions that role needs the dashboard to support.
Dependencies: Role Inventory (DW-B1), Module Inventory (DW-B2).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A role with clear operational responsibility (e.g., ‘Support Agent’) has no dashboard; a dashboard exists with metrics that trace to no underlying data entity.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Metrics with no traceable data entity are routed to Database/Data Discovery (DW-B9) to confirm the entity exists or should be added.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss checks role-to-dashboard coverage specifically, since an operationally necessary role with no dashboard is a common, easily-missed gap.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team challenges whether each dashboard’s metrics are the metrics that role actually needs, not merely metrics that are easy to compute.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A dashboard metric with no backing entity triggers re-entry into Database/Data Discovery.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: REPORT-000n nodes cite the ENTITY-000n nodes (DW-B9) their metrics are computed from.
DW-B8. Form Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover every form and its fields across the product.
Inputs: Page Inventory (DW-B5); Feature Inventory (DW-B3).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Form Inventory, tagged PAGE-00nn-FORM-0n, each with a field list.
Evidence Required: Each field cites the entity attribute it maps to (Database/Data Discovery, DW-B9) or is flagged as computed/derived.
Dependencies: Page Inventory (DW-B5); coordinates with Database/Data Discovery (DW-B9).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A field with no corresponding entity attribute and no ‘derived’ justification; an entity attribute with no form that can set it.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Unmapped fields returned to DW-B8/DW-B9 jointly for reconciliation before Validation Discovery (DW-C4) can run against the form.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss cross-checks field-to-entity-attribute coverage bidirectionally, not just form-by-form.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team checks specifically for missing bulk-entry and import-based alternatives to manual form entry, where genome-typical for the category.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Every certified form becomes a required input to Validation Discovery (DW-C4).
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: PAGE-00nn-FORM-0n nodes are the direct parent of VALIDATION-00nn-0n nodes.
DW-B9. Database/Data Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover every database entity, relationship, and data dependency the product requires.
Inputs: Feature Inventory (DW-B3); Form Inventory (DW-B8); Dashboard Inventory (DW-B7).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Entity Inventory, tagged ENTITY-000n, with relationships to other entities.
Evidence Required: Each entity cites the feature(s) that create, read, update, or delete it.
Dependencies: Feature Inventory (DW-B3).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: An entity with no CRUD consumer anywhere in the Feature Inventory (orphan, Pass 20); a relationship implied by two entities’ fields but not modeled (e.g., Customer and Order with no explicit relationship entity).
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Orphan entities are reparented to a feature or rejected; unmodeled relationships are returned for explicit definition.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss independently derives the expected entity set from the genome-typical data model for this category before diffing by ENTITY- ID.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team checks for missing audit/history entities (e.g., no ‘OrderStatusHistory’ behind an Order entity with a status field).
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A newly required entity (from a Red Team or Negative-Space finding) re-triggers API Discovery (DW-B10) for its CRUD surface.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: ENTITY-000n nodes are required citations for every API-000n, VALIDATION-, and REPORT- node.
DW-B10. API Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover every API and event the product requires.
Inputs: Entity Inventory (DW-B9); Interaction Discovery Worker (DW-C2) preliminary findings.
Outputs / Artifacts Created: API Inventory, tagged API-000n, each mapped to the entity/entities it operates on and the interaction(s) that call it.
Evidence Required: Each API cites the entity it operates on and the calling interaction; each event cites its trigger and its subscriber(s).
Dependencies: Entity Inventory (DW-B9).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: An entity with no full CRUD API surface and no documented reason (e.g., read-only by design); an interaction that implies an API call with no corresponding API artifact.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Missing API coverage is returned to DW-B10 with the specific entity/operation gap identified.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss checks entity-to-API coverage completeness as a matrix, not a count — every entity’s expected operation set (create/read/update/delete/list/search as applicable) is checked individually.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team checks specifically for missing bulk/batch API operations and missing webhook/event surfaces for integration-heavy categories.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A newly discovered API can imply a new Integration Discovery (DW-B11) target if it exists to serve an external consumer.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: API-000n nodes are the primary bridge between the Master Product Inventory and Stage 2 Build artifacts (see §2).
DW-B11. Integration Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover every third-party and internal integration the product requires.
Inputs: Business Model Brief (DW-A4); API Inventory (DW-B10); Domain Profile (DW-A2).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Integration Inventory, tagged INTEGRATION-000n, each with its purpose, direction (inbound/outbound), and the entities it touches.
Evidence Required: Each integration cites either a direct user statement, a business-model dependency (e.g., a stated payment processor), or a domain-standard integration for this category (e.g., email delivery).
Dependencies: Business Model Brief (DW-A4); API Inventory (DW-B10).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A business-model dependency (e.g., subscription billing) with no corresponding integration artifact; an integration with no failure-handling requirement flagged to Edge-Case Discovery.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Missing integrations reopen Pass 13 (Dependency Discovery) for the specific commercial dependency.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss cross-checks every Business Model Brief dependency against the Integration Inventory line by line.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team specifically checks for integration failure/degradation handling (what happens when the payment processor times out) as a Negative-Space consequence.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Every integration triggers a mandatory Edge-Case Discovery sweep (DW-D1) for its failure modes.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: INTEGRATION-000n nodes are required inputs to Operational Discovery (DW-D5) and the Stage 4 deployment verification chain.
Cluster C — Behavioral Discovery Workers
Purpose: determine how the structural inventory behaves.
DW-C1. Page Content Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover every content region within each page — cards, tables, lists, charts, status indicators, and information blocks — as Layer 2 of Recursive Page Expansion (§0.6).
Inputs: Page Inventory (DW-B5); Entity Inventory (DW-B9).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Content Inventory, tagged PAGE-00nn-SECTION-0n-CONTENT-0nn.
Evidence Required: Each content item cites the entity/data it displays and the purpose it serves on that page.
Dependencies: Page Inventory (DW-B5).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A page’s stated purpose (from DW-B5) implies a content region that is not present (e.g., a ‘Customer Details’ page with no activity history despite Activity being a genome-typical feature for this category).
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Missing content regions reopen Layer 2 for the specific page only.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss independently derives the expected content inventory per page from its stated purpose and entity data before diffing by ID — this is the artifact class most exposed to the ‘more items ≠ more complete’ failure mode (§0.16), so the Boss checks Identity and Quality dimensions, not only Count.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team challenges content completeness specifically against the Industry Comparison Engine’s findings for equivalent pages in mature products.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Every certified content item proceeds to Interaction Discovery (DW-C2) for the actions available on it.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: CONTENT-0nn nodes are the most numerous class of artifact in the Master Product Inventory and the primary subject of the worked Boss-reconstruction example in §1.4.
DW-C2. Interaction Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover every user action available on every content item, form, and page — Layer 3 of Recursive Page Expansion.
Inputs: Content Inventory (DW-C1); Business Rule Discovery Worker (DW-C5) preliminary findings.
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Interaction Inventory, tagged ACTION-0nn-0n, from the standing action vocabulary (create, edit, delete, search, filter, sort, export, import, upload, download, approve, reject, archive, restore, assign, invite, cancel, retry) plus category-specific actions.
Evidence Required: Each action cites the permission tier it requires (pending confirmation from Permission Discovery, DW-C6) and the state transition it causes.
Dependencies: Content Inventory (DW-C1).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A destructive action has no corresponding confirmation Modal (cross-checked against DW-B6); an action is listed with no defined resulting State.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Actions with no defined resulting state are returned jointly to DW-C2 and State Discovery (DW-C3).
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss checks specifically that the action set matches what the page’s purpose and role permissions imply — an Interaction Inventory that is merely ‘view + edit’ on a page whose role has approval authority is rejected.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team specifically hunts for missing bulk-action variants (bulk-approve, bulk-export) on list/table content.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Every certified action proceeds to State Discovery (DW-C3) and Validation Discovery (DW-C4).
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: ACTION-0nn-0n nodes are the parent of their corresponding STATE- and VALIDATION- nodes.
DW-C3. State Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover every relevant state per surface and action — Layer 4 of Recursive Page Expansion.
Inputs: Interaction Inventory (DW-C2); Edge-Case Discovery Worker (DW-D1) preliminary findings.
Outputs / Artifacts Created: State Inventory, tagged STATE-0nn-0n-0n, drawn from the standing state vocabulary (initial, loading, empty, success, error, unauthorized, forbidden, not-found, disabled, processing, pending, expired, offline, partially-failed, retrying, locked, archived) filtered to what is relevant.
Evidence Required: Each state cites the specific action or condition that produces it; states are never added by default without a triggering condition.
Dependencies: Interaction Inventory (DW-C2).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: An action with an async/network dependency has no loading or error state defined; a state is defined that no action or condition can actually produce (over-inclusion, penalized under §0.16 Quality/Duplication).
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Missing async states are returned to DW-C3; unreachable states are removed and logged as a self-verification correction.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss checks state coverage against the action’s technical profile (does it call an API? touch a third-party integration? per DW-B10/DW-B11) rather than applying a blanket state template to every action.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team specifically probes for concurrency and partial-failure states (two users editing the same record, a bulk action that partially succeeds).
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Every certified error/failure state is cross-checked against Edge-Case Discovery (DW-D1) for a matching entry, and vice versa.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: STATE- nodes are required inputs to Stage 3 Testing’s derived coverage requirement (§3).
DW-C4. Validation Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover every validation rule, client- and server-side — Layer 5 of Recursive Page Expansion.
Inputs: Form Inventory (DW-B8); Entity Inventory (DW-B9); Business Rule Discovery Worker (DW-C5) preliminary findings.
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Validation Inventory, tagged VALIDATION-00nn-0n: required fields, format rules, duplicate checks, cross-field rules, server/business/permission/state validation.
Evidence Required: Each validation rule cites the field(s) or entity constraint it enforces and whether it is client-side, server-side, or both.
Dependencies: Form Inventory (DW-B8).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A field marked required in the Entity Inventory has no corresponding validation rule; a cross-field business rule (e.g., ‘end date must be after start date’) is not represented as a validation.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Missing validations reopen Layer 5 for the specific form.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss cross-checks every required entity attribute against the Validation Inventory as a matrix, the same discipline as DW-B10’s API coverage check.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team specifically probes for missing server-side validation duplicating client-side rules (a common production vulnerability, not merely a completeness gap).
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A validation rule discovered here that reveals a previously-unmodeled business rule re-triggers Business Rule Discovery (DW-C5).
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: VALIDATION- nodes are required test-case inputs for Stage 3 Testing’s derived coverage.
DW-C5. Business Rule Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover the rules governing every action and state across the product — Layer 6 of Recursive Page Expansion.
Inputs: Interaction Inventory (DW-C2); State Inventory (DW-C3); Domain Profile (DW-A2).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Business Rule Inventory, tagged RULE-000n, each citing the action(s)/state(s) it governs.
Evidence Required: Each rule cites either a user statement, a domain-standard convention, or an explicit inference — inferred rules are flagged for user confirmation via a Discovery Question if consequential (e.g., cancellation windows, refund eligibility).
Dependencies: Interaction Inventory (DW-C2), Domain Profile (DW-A2).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: An approval workflow (from DW-B4) has no rule defining who can approve or under what condition; two rules apply to the same action with contradictory conditions.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Contradictions are routed immediately to the Contradiction Engine (§0.19, DW-E2) rather than resolved unilaterally by this worker.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss specifically checks that every Workflow (DW-B4) with an approval or gating step has a governing rule, since ungoverned gates are a common silent gap.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team specifically probes for rule interactions across roles (does a rule that applies to ‘Manager’ correctly account for a user who holds two roles?).
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A newly discovered rule can imply a new Permission (DW-C6) or a new State (DW-C3, e.g., ‘pending approval’).
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: RULE-000n nodes are cited by every ACTION- and STATE- node they govern, and are the primary input to the Contradiction Engine (§0.19).
DW-C6. Permission Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover who may view, create, edit, delete, approve, reject, export, or access sensitive data or administrative actions, per surface — Layer 7 of Recursive Page Expansion.
Inputs: Role Inventory (DW-B1); Interaction Inventory (DW-C2); Business Rule Inventory (DW-C5).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Permission Inventory, tagged PERMISSION-000n, mapped ROLE-000n × ACTION-0nn-0n.
Evidence Required: Each permission grant or denial cites either a user statement or a role-capability-boundary inference from the Role Inventory (DW-B1); sensitive-data access is always evidenced by a citation to a Security Discovery (DW-D2) finding.
Dependencies: Role Inventory (DW-B1), Interaction Inventory (DW-C2).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: An action exists with no permission mapping for any role (silently allowing or silently blocking everyone, both errors); two roles are granted contradictory access to the same sensitive action.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Contradictory grants are routed to the Contradiction Engine (§0.19); unmapped actions are returned to DW-C6.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss reconstructs the expected permission matrix independently from the Role Inventory’s stated capability boundaries before diffing cell-by-cell against the actual Permission Inventory — not spot-checked, but checked as a full matrix.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team’s standing example finding (see §0.19 worked example): a business rule and a permission entry that contradict each other on who may delete a resource.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Every certified permission is a required input to Security Discovery (DW-D2).
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: PERMISSION-000n nodes are required verification inputs for Stage 3 Testing’s permission-testing coverage (§3) and are audited every time a Safe Change Intelligence (§5) impact analysis touches an existing action.
DW-C7. Notification Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover every notification, its trigger, and its recipients.
Inputs: Workflow Inventory (DW-B4); State Inventory (DW-C3); Business Rule Inventory (DW-C5).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Notification Inventory, tagged NOTIFICATION-000n: trigger event, recipient role(s), channel, and content summary.
Evidence Required: Each notification cites the triggering state transition or rule and the role(s) that need to know about it.
Dependencies: Workflow Inventory (DW-B4), State Inventory (DW-C3).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: An approval-chain workflow step completes with no notification to the next approver; a failure state (payment failure, integration failure) has no user-facing notification.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Missing notifications on approval or failure transitions are treated as a Priority-1 gap and reopen this pass immediately rather than waiting for the next full sweep.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss specifically checks every multi-role Workflow (DW-B4) for a complete handoff-notification chain, since a missing notification silently breaks a workflow that otherwise looks complete.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team specifically checks failure and expiration states (DW-C3) for missing notifications, since these are more often forgotten than success-path notifications.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A notification discovered here can imply a new Settings/Preferences feature (notification preferences) not yet in the Feature Inventory — routed back to DW-B3.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: NOTIFICATION-000n nodes are required inputs to Operational Discovery (DW-D5) for delivery-channel monitoring.
DW-C8. Dependency Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover everything one artifact implies about another across the entire Master Product Inventory — the cross-cutting dependency map, run as Pass 13.
Inputs: The full Master Product Inventory as certified through Pass 12.
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Dependency Map, cross-referencing every artifact’s depends_on / depended_on_by metadata fields (§0.13).
Evidence Required: Each dependency edge cites the specific mechanism of dependence (e.g., ‘ACTION-0042-03 depends on INTEGRATION-0007 because it calls the payment processor’).
Dependencies: All Cluster B and Cluster C workers’ certified output through Pass 12.
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A dependency edge exists in one direction but not the reciprocal (depends_on set without the corresponding depended_on_by); a dependency chain terminates in an artifact that does not exist.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Broken or one-directional edges are returned to the artifact’s owning worker for correction, since the Dependency Discovery Worker records dependencies, it does not resolve gaps on other workers’ artifacts.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss spot-audits dependency chains by walking them end-to-end for a sample of high-fan-out artifacts (e.g., a core Entity referenced by dozens of Actions).
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team specifically looks for circular dependencies and single points of failure (one Integration that half the Feature Inventory silently depends on with no fallback).
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A broken dependency chain discovered here is one of the required exit criteria for Pass 21 (Recursive Re-Discovery) — the sweep cannot close while any chain is broken.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: The Dependency Map is the machine-checkable backbone of the Application Knowledge Graph’s ‘nothing exists in isolation’ guarantee, first stated in the original architecture’s §0.2.
Cluster D — Non-Functional Discovery Workers
Purpose: determine what the product must satisfy beyond its stated functionality.
DW-D1. Edge-Case Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Run the standing edge-case question set (§0.8) against every feature, page, action, and workflow to surface failure and abnormal conditions.
Inputs: Feature Inventory (DW-B3), Interaction Inventory (DW-C2), Integration Inventory (DW-B11).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Edge-Case Register, tagged EDGECASE-000n, each routed to the artifact type it actually implies (state, dialog, notification, rule, or new page).
Evidence Required: Each edge case cites the specific standing question (§0.8) it answers and the artifact it was routed to.
Dependencies: Interaction Inventory (DW-C2); Integration Inventory (DW-B11) for third-party failure modes.
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A payment-bearing workflow has no entry for payment failure/pending/reversal; a multi-step workflow has no entry for abandonment mid-flow.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Missing edge cases for payment, concurrency, and abandonment are treated as Priority-1 and block Pass 12 exit specifically, not just the general sweep.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss independently applies the standing question set to the highest-risk workflows (payment, irreversible actions, multi-step processes) before comparing to this worker’s register, mirroring the §0.15 reconstruction discipline at the edge-case level.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Works in tandem with the Discovery Red Team (DW-E4): this worker runs the standing question set systematically, the Red Team probes for edge cases the standing set itself doesn’t anticipate.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Every routed edge case (a new state, dialog, or notification) re-enters the relevant Cluster C worker’s pass.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: EDGECASE-000n entries are required inputs to Stage 3 Testing’s failure-condition coverage (§3).
DW-D2. Security Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover security boundaries, sensitive operations, authorization requirements, and injection/abuse risk across the product.
Inputs: Permission Inventory (DW-C6); API Inventory (DW-B10); Entity Inventory (DW-B9).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Security Requirement Set, tagged SECURITY-000n: authentication requirements, sensitive-data handling rules, audit requirements, session behavior.
Evidence Required: Each requirement cites the specific artifact (API, entity field, or action) it constrains.
Dependencies: Permission Inventory (DW-C6); API Inventory (DW-B10).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: An API that writes a sensitive entity field has no audit-logging requirement attached; a session-bound action has no session-expiry behavior defined.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Sensitive-data APIs with no audit requirement are treated as Priority-1 and block Pass 14 exit.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss independently derives the expected security requirement set from the Permission Inventory plus the Foundational Intelligence Layer’s regulatory/security patterns for this domain (reasoned directly, not through the Domain Profile) before diffing — the Domain Profile’s compliance flags are themselves part of what gets checked, not part of what the Boss reasons from.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Primary Red Team collaboration point: the Red Team explicitly probes this worker’s output for unhandled sensitive operations and privilege-boundary gaps.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A newly discovered sensitive operation re-triggers Permission Discovery (DW-C6) confirmation.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: SECURITY-000n entries are mandatory inputs to Stage 2 Build’s implementation review (§2) and Stage 3 Testing’s security-testing coverage (§3).
DW-D3. Accessibility Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover keyboard, screen-reader, and semantic requirements per surface — Layer 9 of Recursive Page Expansion.
Inputs: Content Inventory (DW-C1); Interaction Inventory (DW-C2).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Accessibility Requirement Set, tagged A11Y-00nn-0n: keyboard navigation path, focus management, labeling, and screen-reader behavior per interactive element.
Evidence Required: Each requirement cites the specific interactive content element it governs.
Dependencies: Content Inventory (DW-C1), Interaction Inventory (DW-C2).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: An interactive element (custom dropdown, modal, drag-and-drop) has no keyboard-equivalent interaction defined; a dynamic content update has no screen-reader announcement requirement.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Missing keyboard-equivalents for custom interactive components are Priority-1 and block Pass 14 exit.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss spot-checks accessibility coverage on every custom (non-standard-form) interactive component specifically, since standard form fields inherit baseline accessibility from the Product DNA Library while custom components do not.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team checks for accessibility requirements on modals and dynamic content (DW-B6, DW-C3 states) specifically, the most commonly under-specified category.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Not typically a source of new structural artifacts; findings attach directly to existing Content/Interaction nodes.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: A11Y- entries are mandatory inputs to Stage 3 Testing’s accessibility-testing coverage (§3).
DW-D4. Performance Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover loading, pagination, caching, and scale expectations per surface — Layer 12 of Recursive Page Expansion.
Inputs: Content Inventory (DW-C1); Entity Inventory (DW-B9) with expected record-volume signals from the Domain Profile.
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Performance Requirement Set, tagged PERF-00nn-0n: pagination/virtualization requirement, caching strategy, and expensive-operation flags.
Evidence Required: Each requirement cites the specific content list or operation it constrains and the volume assumption it is based on.
Dependencies: Content Inventory (DW-C1); Entity Inventory (DW-B9).
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A list-type content region backed by an entity with no volume ceiling has no pagination or virtualization requirement; an expensive operation (bulk export, report generation) has no async/background-job requirement.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Missing pagination requirements on unbounded lists are treated as Priority-1.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss checks every list-type Content node against its backing Entity for a stated or inferred volume assumption before accepting the absence of a pagination requirement as correct.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team checks specifically for missing caching/rate-limit requirements on API endpoints exposed to bulk operations.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A flagged expensive operation can imply a new background-job Feature not yet in the Feature Inventory — routed to DW-B3.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: PERF- entries are mandatory inputs to Stage 3 Testing’s performance-testing coverage (§3) and Stage 4’s capacity monitoring (§4).
DW-D5. Operational Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover monitoring, logging, auditing, backup, and recovery needs across the product.

Rejection / Rework Behavior: Missing monitoring on Priority-1 integrations blocks Pass 15 exit.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss independently derives the expected operational baseline for this category from the Product DNA Library before diffing against DW-D5’s output — this is the specification Stage 4 (§4) is later built and verified against.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team checks specifically for missing disaster-recovery requirements on any entity the Business Model Brief (DW-A4) identifies as commercially critical.

DW-D6. Compliance Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Discover applicable regulatory and standards requirements for this product.

DW-D7. Documentation Discovery Worker

Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team checks specifically for missing admin/operator documentation, the most commonly deprioritized documentation class.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Not generative of new structural artifacts.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: DOC-000n entries are mandatory inputs to Blueprint Completeness Certification (§0.23).
Cluster E — Analysis & Adversarial Workers
Purpose: attack the inventory the other four clusters produced.
DW-E1. Completeness Analysis Worker
Responsibility: Run Recursive Self-Discovery (Passes 22 within the department’s own sweep, ahead of Boss reconstruction) — repeatedly ask ‘what is still missing?’ across the full Master Product Inventory.
Inputs: The full Master Product Inventory as certified through Pass 21.
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Completeness Findings, each either a new Discovery Question or a routed artifact gap assigned back to its owning worker.
Evidence Required: Each finding cites the specific completeness question (from the standing list in §0.5/Pass 22: what have we forgotten, what would a senior PM/architect/UX/QA/DevOps/Security expect) that surfaced it.
Dependencies: Certified output of all Cluster A–D workers through Pass 21.
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A completeness sweep produces zero findings on the first attempt — treated with suspicion, not confidence, and triggers a second sweep from a different question ordering before Pass 22 is allowed to close.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Not itself reworked; it routes gaps to the owning worker and re-sweeps after correction.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Feeds directly into the Discovery Boss’s own independent reconstruction (§0.15) as one input among several — the Boss does not defer to this worker’s findings, it re-derives its own expectation independently and treats agreement or disagreement with this worker’s findings as informative, not authoritative.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Operates ahead of the Red Team in the pass sequence (Pass 22 vs. Pass 17) but the Red Team’s later findings are cross-checked against this worker’s completeness sweep for consistency.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Is, itself, the mechanism of Pass 21’s recursive loop: a sweep that produces new findings triggers another sweep.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: Does not create new artifact types; its findings become new instances of existing types (a new PAGE-, a new RULE-, etc.) once routed and certified.
DW-E2. Contradiction Analysis Worker
Responsibility: Run the Contradiction Engine (§0.19) — continuously scan the Master Product Inventory for direct and structural contradictions.
Inputs: The full Master Product Inventory, continuously as it grows (not only at Pass 19).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Contradiction Register, tagged CONTRADICTION-000n, each citing both conflicting artifacts by ID.
Evidence Required: Each entry cites the exact two (or more) artifacts in conflict and the specific dimension of conflict (permission vs. rule, rule vs. rule, state vs. workflow).
Dependencies: Runs against Business Rule Inventory (DW-C5), Permission Inventory (DW-C6), and Workflow Inventory (DW-B4) primarily, but scans all clusters.
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A contradiction is silently resolved by picking one side without logging the conflict and its resolution rationale — this is itself treated as a verification failure of this worker, not a legitimate resolution.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: This worker never resolves a contradiction unilaterally; it routes to the Discovery Boss (structural/product contradictions) or the Multi-Perspective Reasoning Council (judgment contradictions) and holds the entry open until a resolution with rationale is recorded.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: The Discovery Boss cannot certify Pass 19 (or issue Blueprint Completeness Certification, §0.23) while any Contradiction Register entry is open.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Red Team is a major source of raw material for this worker — many Red Team findings are, at root, contradictions between what one artifact implies and what another states.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: A resolved contradiction that changes an artifact re-triggers that artifact’s downstream dependency chain (via the Dependency Map, DW-C8) for re-verification.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: The Contradiction Register’s open-entry count is a required-zero gate for Blueprint Completeness Certification (§0.23), not merely a report.
DW-E3. Negative-Space Discovery Worker
Responsibility: Run Negative-Space Discovery (§0.11) — ask, recursively, what should exist because something else already exists.
Inputs: The full Master Product Inventory, focused on Cluster B (Structural) artifacts as the seed set.
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Negative-Space Findings, each resolved to Required/Not-Required/Uncertain exactly as the Industry Comparison Engine resolves its findings (§0.9).
Evidence Required: Each finding cites the seed artifact (e.g., a Payments feature) and the specific implied-ecosystem member it investigated (e.g., refunds).
Dependencies: Feature Inventory (DW-B3) and Integration Inventory (DW-B11) as primary seed sets.
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A finding is added to the inventory without being evaluated against this specific product’s actual scope (defaulting to ‘add everything the ecosystem could contain’).
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Un-scoped additions are returned and re-evaluated against the Product Understanding Brief (DW-A1) before being accepted.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: Boss checks that every ‘Required’ resolution from this worker actually traces to a real product need, not merely ecosystem completeness for its own sake — this is one of the specific failure modes named in the adversarial self-review (§Adversarial Review).
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Works in tandem with the Red Team: this worker reasons forward from what exists to what it implies; the Red Team reasons adversarially about what the current inventory as a whole might still be missing.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Any ‘Required’ finding re-enters Page/Feature/Workflow Discovery and can itself become a new seed for further negative-space reasoning — the recursive property described in §0.11.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: Findings become ordinary instances of existing artifact types once accepted; the worker itself creates no new artifact class.
DW-E4. Discovery Red Team
Responsibility: Actively attack the certified inventory under the standing charter: assume it is incomplete, find what everyone else forgot (§0.10).
Inputs: The full Master Product Inventory as certified through Pass 16 (after Industry Comparison).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Red Team Findings, tagged REDTEAM-000n, resolved to Missing (added)/Not-Required (documented)/Uncertain (investigated).
Evidence Required: Each finding cites the specific category challenged (module, feature, page, workflow, permission, edge case, etc.) and the reasoning for why it is expected.
Dependencies: Full inventory through Pass 16.
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: Findings that merely propose additional functionality without evidence that it is genuinely missing (volume without genuine omission) — this worker’s internal scoring explicitly does not reward volume, only substantiated gaps.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: Findings resolved ‘Not-Required’ still require a documented reason; an unresolved ‘Uncertain’ finding cannot be closed without a Discovery Question or a Boss ruling.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: The Discovery Boss treats every ‘Missing’ finding from the Red Team as a required delta in its own reconstruction (§0.15) — a Red Team finding the Boss’s independent reconstruction did not also surface is itself investigated as a possible gap in the Boss’s own model.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: N/A (this is the Red Team itself).
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: Every accepted ‘Missing’ finding re-enters the relevant Discovery pass exactly as any other new artifact would.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: Red Team findings are the only artifact class explicitly scored for genuine-omission rate as an ongoing quality metric on the Worker Corps as a whole, feeding the Continuous Learning Engine (see Cross-Cutting).
DW-E5. Discovery Auditor
Responsibility: Perform the final, independent re-check after the Discovery Boss has certified the inventory (Pass 24) — the last gate before Blueprint Completeness Certification is eligible to be evaluated by Stage 1.
Inputs: The Discovery Boss’s certified Master Product Inventory and its reconstruction-diff record (§0.15).
Outputs / Artifacts Created: Audit Certificate: either a clean certification or a rejection with the specific artifacts and dimensions (of the eleven in §0.16) that failed.
Evidence Required: The Auditor independently samples a statistically meaningful subset of artifacts across every cluster and re-runs the evidence check (§0.18) on each, rather than reviewing the Boss’s summary.
Dependencies: Discovery Boss’s Pass 23 output.
Verification: Self-verification against §0.14, then Specialist Verifier review of evidence per §0.18 (never narrative alone).
Failure Conditions: A sampled artifact fails evidence inspection despite carrying a CERTIFIED status — this is treated as a Priority-1 failure of the certification process itself, not merely of the one artifact, and triggers a wider re-sample.
Rejection / Rework Behavior: On any sampled failure, the Auditor widens its sample and, if the failure rate exceeds the department’s tolerance, rejects the entire certification and returns it to the Discovery Boss rather than approving with exceptions.
Interaction with the Discovery Boss: The Auditor is structurally independent of the Discovery Boss — the same separation of production and judgment described in §0.4 applies a second time here, since the Boss’s own judgment is itself audited, not merely the workers’.
Interaction with Red Team / Auditor: Distinct from the Red Team: the Red Team attacks completeness before certification; the Auditor verifies the certification process itself was sound after the fact.
Contribution to Recursive Discovery: An Auditor rejection re-enters the department at Pass 23 (Boss reconstruction), not Pass 1 — the Auditor’s finding is about the certification, not necessarily about needing to re-run all of Discovery.
Contribution to the Master Product Inventory: Only after a clean Audit Certificate does the Master Product Inventory become eligible to be read as the frozen baseline for Stage 1 Design’s own independent reconstruction (§1).
0.4 The Worker → Verifier → Boss → Independent Audit → Certification Model
This is the platform’s single governance pattern, introduced here and reused unmodified in Stages 1 through 5.
WORKER produces an artifact and evidence of how it was produced
   ↓
SELF-VERIFICATION — the worker checks its own output against §0.14&#39;s checklist
   ↓
SPECIALIST VERIFIER — a paired, independent role checks the evidence, not the explanation
   ↓
BOSS — independently reconstructs the expected result and compares (§0.15)
   ↓
INDEPENDENT AUDIT — a role uninvolved in production or the Boss&#39;s reconstruction re-checks
   ↓
CERTIFICATION — the artifact&#39;s state advances (§0.17)
Three rules make this more than a diagram:
The worker never certifies its own work. A worker declaring “done” changes nothing about the artifact’s state.
The Specialist Verifier checks evidence, not narrative. It does not ask the worker to explain why the work is correct; it independently inspects what the count actually represents (§0.16, §0.18).
The Boss reconstructs before it compares. It does not read the worker’s inventory and nod along — it builds its own independent expectation first, exactly as specified in §0.15, and only then diffs the two.
Boss independence means independence from the producing workers and their claims, not independence from the certified upstream baseline. Each Boss independently reconstructs the expected output of its stage using the authoritative certified inputs for that stage — for Discovery, the raw Product Understanding Brief and the platform’s trusted foundational knowledge, since nothing certified yet exists upstream of Discovery; for every later stage, the certified baseline the prior stage’s own Boss and Independent Audit already certified, plus that stage’s trusted domain knowledge — and only then compares that independent expectation against what the current stage’s own Worker Corps actually produced. A Boss never treats the current stage’s worker conclusions, inventories, explanations, or completion claims as ground truth; it always treats an already-certified upstream artifact as legitimate authoritative input. §0.15 states this precisely for the Discovery Boss, and §1.3, §2.3, §3.3, §4.2, §4.5, and §5.2 state the equivalent for every later stage.

0.6 Recursive Page Expansion — the 14 Layers
A page is not a single artifact — it is the parent of everything a real, production page must define. Every page in the Page Inventory is recursively expanded through the following layers before it is eligible for Design. The system is required to determine what is relevant per layer, not to apply every possible item to every page indiscriminately — a settings page does not need the same interaction inventory as a transaction table, and inventing irrelevant items is treated as a Contradiction Engine finding (§0.19), not as thoroughness.

Scale is a consequence of correctness, not a target: if a page genuinely decomposes into eighty tracked requirements, eighty are recorded; if it decomposes into two hundred, two hundred are recorded. Every recorded item is additionally classified as required, optional, duplicate, unnecessary, derived, supporting, conflicting, or deprecated — a long inventory full of invented or duplicate items is scored as a failure on the Quality and Duplication dimensions of §0.16, not credited as thoroughness.
0.7 Feature Family Expansion (Feature Expansion Engine, Strengthened)
The Feature Expansion Engine, preserved from the original architecture, is formalized here as a driver of Pass 11. For every discovered feature, the engine asks what a complete, production version of that feature family requires — supporting pages, workflows, reports, permissions, APIs, database relationships, and business processes — and generates Discovery Questions for anything the answer depends on information the platform does not yet have (rather than guessing). This is the same operative pattern as Negative-Space Discovery (§0.11) applied specifically at the feature level rather than the whole-product level.
0.8 Edge-Case Discovery — a Dedicated Discipline
For every feature, page, action, and workflow, the Edge-Case Discovery Worker evaluates a standing question set covering: success and failure paths; empty, invalid, unauthorized, and unauthenticated data or access; expiration (of sessions, tokens, offers, holds); payment failure, pending, and reversal states where applicable; network, API, database, and third-party service failure; timeouts; missing or already-deleted objects; concurrent modification; duplicate submission; abandoned multi-step workflows; unavailable dependencies; unexpected external events; and partial failure. Each answer is routed to the artifact type it actually implies — a content region, a component, a state, a dialog, a flow, a recovery mechanism, a page, a workflow, a notification, a business rule, a permission, an API, or a data relationship — rather than defaulting to “add a new page” for every finding.
0.9 The Industry Comparison Engine
The Industry Pattern Research Worker’s findings are evaluated by a dedicated Industry Comparison Engine whose purpose is completeness discovery, not imitation: what do mature products in this category normally contain that the current inventory may have overlooked? — drawing on mature product patterns, established workflows, common user expectations, administrative and operational patterns, industry conventions, common failure handling, common settings, common reports, and common permissions. Every discovered possibility is evaluated against this specific project’s actual purpose and resolved to exactly one of three outcomes: Required → added to the inventory; Not Required → recorded with a stated reason; Uncertain → converted into a Discovery Question for the user. Competitor features are never copied by default — the engine exists to find omissions, not to clone competitors.
0.10 Adversarial Discovery — the Discovery Red Team
The Discovery Red Team’s explicit mission is stated as a standing charter: assume the current inventory is incomplete; find what everyone else forgot. It actively challenges modules, features, pages, sub-pages, workflows, content, actions, states, validations, permissions, business rules, integrations, APIs, database entities, settings, notifications, edge cases, administrative and operational functionality, security, accessibility, scalability, and recovery — the same categories the Worker Corps produced, examined adversarially rather than constructively. Every challenge resolves to Missing → added; Not Required → documented with reason; Uncertain → investigated — and the Red Team’s compensation model (in the platform’s internal scoring, not a human incentive) rewards genuine omissions found, never volume of invented functionality proposed.
0.11 Negative-Space Discovery
A dedicated mechanism asks, recursively: what should exist because something else already exists? If Payments exists, the Negative-Space Discovery Worker investigates the ecosystem a payments capability implies — success, failure, pending, timeout, reversal, refund, dispute, receipt, history, permissions, notifications, records, reconciliation, and admin visibility — and evaluates each candidate against this product’s actual scope rather than adding all of them by default. This runs recursively: any capability accepted into the inventory as a consequence of negative-space reasoning can itself trigger another round of negative-space investigation, bounded by the same Pass 21 re-discovery loop that governs all recursive expansion.

0.14 Discovery Self-Verification
Before any artifact is handed to a Specialist Verifier, its producing worker must attempt to disprove its own completeness, answering a standing checklist: What did we forget? What did we assume? What dependency or supporting feature is missing? Every uncertainty surfaced here is recorded — never silently resolved by guessing — and either becomes a Risk & Assumption Register entry (§0.20) or a Discovery Question routed back to the user.
0.15 The Discovery Boss — Independent Reconstruction
Worker Discovery and Boss Independent Reconstruction are two genuinely separate reasoning paths, not one path read twice. The Discovery Boss does not review the Worker Corps’s inventory by reading it, and — critically — it does not build its own expectation from worker-produced conclusions either. Domain Profile (DW-A2), Category Classification (DW-A5), and every other worker-generated artifact are themselves claims requiring independent verification, not inputs the Boss is entitled to inherit as settled fact. If the Boss reasoned from those conclusions, an error made early in the Worker Corps’s own reasoning (a misclassified domain, a wrong category) would simply be re-confirmed by a Boss that unknowingly inherited it, rather than caught.
Instead, the Boss reasons from only two sources: the raw Product Understanding Brief / original user vision (DW-A1’s output, which is a direct paraphrase of what the user actually said, not an inference), and the platform’s trusted foundational knowledge — the Application Intelligence System, Product DNA Library, and Application Genome Engine’s permanent, cross-project taxonomy (§0.2), consulted directly rather than through any worker’s filtered application of it. From these two sources alone, the Boss independently determines, before it looks at a single Worker Corps output:
what type of product this is
what the product is intended to accomplish
what major capabilities should exist
what major pages, modules, and workflows are likely required
what domain patterns apply
what important negative-space functionality may exist
Only after this independent expectation is fully constructed does the Boss inspect the Worker Corps’s actual inventory — including its Domain Profile, Category Classification, and every downstream artifact — and diff its own expectation against what the workers actually produced:

The Boss is structurally empowered to reject confident-but-incorrect work — confidence expressed by a worker, or the mere existence of a plausible-looking Domain Profile or Category Classification, is never, by itself, evidence.
The general principle, stated once and applied everywhere: Boss independence means independence from the producing workers of its own stage and their claims — it does not mean independence from the certified upstream baseline those workers were building against. Discovery sits at the foundation, so the Discovery Boss has no certified upstream baseline to inherit and reasons instead from the raw Product Understanding Brief and the platform’s trusted foundational knowledge alone, exactly as described above. Every later Boss is different in exactly one respect: it has a certified upstream baseline available — the output of an earlier stage’s own completed Worker → Verifier → Boss → Independent Audit → Certification chain — and that certified baseline is legitimate authoritative input, not a worker conclusion the Boss must route around. What no Boss at any stage may do is treat its own stage’s producing workers’ conclusions, inventories, explanations, or completion claims as ground truth. Applied per stage: the Design Boss (§1.3) reconstructs its expectation from the certified Discovery baseline plus trusted design knowledge, never from the Design Worker Corps’s own design rationale; the Build Boss (§2.3) reconstructs from the certified Design baseline plus trusted engineering knowledge, never from the Build Worker Corps’s claims about what they implemented; the Test Boss (§3.3) reconstructs from the certified Discovery and Design baselines plus trusted testing/security/quality knowledge, never from the Test Worker Corps’s own test counts or claims; the Deployment/Operations Bosses (§4.2, §4.5) reconstruct from certified Build, certified deployment requirements, and the Living Blueprint plus trusted operational knowledge, never from a worker’s status report — verified instead against the actual live system; and the Continuous Engineering Boss (§5.2) reconstructs from the current Living Blueprint plus the change request, never from the Self-Healing System’s own diagnosis of its candidate fix. In every case, the producing workers of that specific stage are exactly who is being verified, so their conclusions can never also be the material their own Boss reasons from — but everything already certified upstream of them is fair, authoritative ground for the Boss’s independent expectation.
0.16 Counts Are Not Proof — the Eleven-Dimension Verification Standard
No certification anywhere in the platform — Discovery, Design, Build, Test, or Operations — may rely on a count match alone. A result with more items than required can still be incomplete; a result with exactly the expected count can still be wrong. Every completeness or correctness claim is checked against all eleven dimensions before certification:
Count — does the quantity match the independently-derived expectation?
Coverage — does every required category have at least the expected representation?
Identity — do the specific items match by ID, not merely by tally?
Correctness — is each item itself accurate?
Quality — does each item meet the department’s minimum bar, not merely exist?
Traceability — does each item trace to a requirement, and a requirement trace to it?
Dependency integrity — are declared dependencies real and satisfied?
Duplication — are there redundant items masquerading as coverage?
Conflicts — do any items contradict one another (routed to §0.19)?
Consistency — does terminology, structure, and pattern hold across the inventory?
Evidence — is there inspectable evidence behind the claim, not only a self-report (§0.18)?
A finding of 199 pages produced against 200 required fails outright, but so does 1,479 contents produced against 1,099 required — the excess must be resolved into matched, missing, extra, duplicate, conflicting, incorrectly-mapped, and unverified before the gate can even be evaluated, exactly as the count-is-not-proof principle requires.
0.17 Definition of Complete — the Artifact State Machine
“Done” is never a worker’s declaration; it is a position in a fixed state machine that every artifact in the Application Knowledge Graph occupies, from first discovery through permanent post-launch operation:
DISCOVERED → EXPANDED → SELF-VERIFIED → SPECIALIST-VERIFIED → BOSS-VERIFIED
    → CERTIFIED → DESIGNED → DESIGN-VERIFIED → IMPLEMENTED → CODE-VERIFIED
    → TESTED → TEST-VERIFIED → CERTIFIED COMPLETE
An artifact can only move to the next state through the gate that governs that transition (self-verification, specialist verification, Boss review, independent audit, or the equivalent gate in Stages 1–5). A worker’s own “this is done” claim changes nothing about state; only a passed gate does. This state machine is what allows the Product Coverage Engine, Blueprint Completeness Certification, and Blueprint Confidence (all preserved from the original architecture, §0.22–0.23) to report a real, artifact-level completeness picture rather than an aggregate confidence number with no underlying structure.
0.18 Evidence-Based Verification
Every completion claim carries inspectable evidence, not a self-reported tally. For a page such as PAGE-0042, the expected shape (37 contents, 12 actions, 8 states, 6 validations, 4 permissions, 3 API relationships) is matched against actual mapped evidence (37/37 contents mapped, 12/12 actions mapped, and so on), and the Specialist Verifier independently inspects what each mapped count actually represents rather than accepting the tally at face value. A worker that reports a matching count without inspectable mapping evidence fails verification regardless of whether the number is correct — an accidentally-correct unverifiable count is not distinguishable, at production time, from a wrong one.
0.19 The Contradiction Engine
A dedicated engine continuously scans the Master Product Inventory for direct and structural contradictions — for example, a business rule stating only administrators can delete users alongside a permission entry granting managers delete-user access. Detected contradictions are never silently resolved by picking one side; each is logged to the Contradiction Register with both conflicting artifacts cited by ID, routed to the Discovery Boss (structural product contradictions) or the Multi-Perspective Reasoning Council (contradictions of judgment, e.g. two specialist perspectives disagreeing on a rule’s correctness), and held open until an explicit resolution is recorded with its rationale. Pass 19 of the Discovery sequence does not clear until the Contradiction Register reaches zero unresolved entries.
0.20 Risk & Assumption Register (Preserved, Strengthened)
Unchanged in purpose from the original architecture: every assumption the platform makes on the user’s behalf, and every risk that assumption introduces if wrong, is captured, owned, and scored. This edition ties every register entry to the artifact metadata schema (§0.13) so that an assumption’s downstream blast radius — every artifact whose confidence depends on it — is queryable by ID rather than left implicit, and requires every entry to be either resolved with evidence or explicitly accepted by the user before Blueprint Completeness Certification (§0.23) can issue.
0.21 Multi-Perspective Reasoning Council (Preserved)
Unchanged from the original architecture: fifteen simulated specialist perspectives — Product Manager, Business Analyst, Software Architect, UX Designer, UI Designer, Backend Engineer, Frontend Engineer, Database Architect, Security Architect, Performance Engineer, DevOps Engineer, QA Engineer, Accessibility Specialist, Compliance Specialist, and Domain Expert — each produce independent findings before merging, with the Executive Orchestrator reconciling disagreement rather than averaging it away. In this edition, Council findings that identify missing or incorrect artifacts are logged with the same artifact-ID and metadata discipline as every other finding in the department, so a Council objection is traceable exactly like a Red Team finding or a Boss-reconstruction delta.
0.22 Requirements Traceability Engine & Design-to-Code Traceability (Preserved)
Unchanged in purpose: every requirement is tracked for life through product requirement, functional requirement, design, components, APIs, database, business logic, tests, and documentation, and every design element maps bidirectionally to the implementation artifacts it produces. This edition makes both engines concrete rather than aspirational by grounding them in the artifact ID system (§0.12) and the state machine (§0.17): a “broken trace” is now a mechanically detectable condition — an ID with a missing edge in the graph — rather than something only discovered by manual review.
0.23 Product Coverage Engine, Blueprint Completeness Certification & Blueprint Confidence (Preserved, Strengthened)

  MISSING:      PAGE-0187-DESIGN (no design artifact exists for certified PAGE-0187)

  EXTRA:        380 CONTENT-DESIGN artifacts with no corresponding certified CONTENT- ID
                 — investigated individually: 210 are duplicates of already-designed content
                 (same CONTENT- ID, redesigned twice under different DESIGN- suffixes),

                 certify and no Discovery Question exists authorizing the addition), and
                 30 are legitimate DESIGN-level decomposition of a single certified CONTENT-
                 artifact into multiple visual elements — permitted, but required to declare

2.3 Build Boss Reconstruction — Method
Identical discipline to §1.3, one stage down the chain: the Build Boss reads the certified DESIGN- inventory, independently derives the expected -IMPL artifact set and behavior per artifact using trusted engineering knowledge (established implementation patterns from the Foundational Intelligence Layer) plus the certified Discovery-level requirement each Design artifact traces back to, then diffs against actual implementation by ID — missing implementations, extra/unauthorized implementations, duplicated logic, and incorrect behavioral mappings are each enumerated individually, never summarized as an aggregate pass/fail. An implementation that handles more cases than the design specified is investigated exactly as an over-produced Design artifact is in §1.3, but resolves differently: the Build Boss has no authority to certify added scope, retroactively or otherwise. Every extra -IMPL artifact or behavior with no corresponding certified -DESIGN artifact is, without exception, classified as one of only two things:
A. Proven to already belong to an existing certified artifact. The Build Worker Corps (or the Build Boss, on review) demonstrates that the extra behavior is genuinely within the bounds of what an already-certified -DESIGN artifact specifies — for example, a validation the Design correctly implied but expressed generally rather than enumerating every case. This is not new scope; it is a documented reading of existing scope, and the Build Boss records the citation to the covering -DESIGN artifact as the artifact’s evidence.
B. An unauthorized deviation, routed to the Change / Reopening Process (§2.4). Anything that cannot be proven to fall under (A) is never certified at the Build stage under any framing — “legitimate extension,” “obviously needed,” or “the design clearly should have included this” are not certification pathways available to a Build worker or the Build Boss. The artifact is rejected, the implementation is held at its pre-deviation state, and a Change/Reopening Request is opened. A coding worker can propose that something is missing; it can never make that proposal true by building it and having it accepted after the fact.
This closes what would otherwise be a scope-expansion path: a Build Boss that could retroactively certify Design-level additions would let a coding worker gradually change the product simply by claiming its own additions were legitimate. A certified Design is the Build contract. Nothing outside that contract reaches production through Stage 2 alone, regardless of how clearly warranted it appears.
2.4 The Change / Reopening Process — No Silent Scope Expansion
When Build (or, by the same rule, Test in §3 or Deployment/Operations in §4) discovers a genuine gap — something the certified upstream baseline does not cover but that the product actually requires — the gap can never be closed by the discovering stage acting alone. It is always routed through a fixed, fail-closed sequence:
STAGE DISCOVERS MISSING REQUIREMENT  (Build, Test, or Deployment/Operations)
   ↓
CHANGE / REOPEN REQUEST — logged against the specific certified artifact(s) it affects,
   or as a new artifact proposal if nothing existing covers it
   ↓
IMPACT ANALYSIS — Safe Change Intelligence (preserved from the original architecture,
   §5.1; the same engine used for post-launch changes governs pre-launch reopening,
   not a separate mechanism) determines what else the change touches: which APIs,
   tables, permissions, workflows, UI components, tests, and integrations are affected
   ↓
DISCOVERY / DESIGN RE-EVALUATION AS REQUIRED — the relevant Discovery worker(s) (§0.3)
   and/or Design specialist(s) (§1.1) re-run against the new information; a genuinely
   new requirement gets a real artifact ID and goes through the same Discovery
   verification chain (§0.4) as anything found during the original Discovery passes
   ↓
DESIGN UPDATED — the affected `-DESIGN` artifact(s) are created or revised
   ↓
DESIGN VERIFICATION — Self-Verification → Specialist Verifier, as in §1.2
   ↓
DESIGN BOSS CERTIFICATION — the Design Boss reconstructs and diffs exactly as in §1.3;
   the added scope is certified on its own merits, not inherited from Build&#39;s claim
   that it was needed
   ↓
BUILD RESUMES — against the newly certified Design baseline
   ↓
BUILD VERIFICATION — as in §2.2, now against the expanded contract
Three properties make this fail-closed rather than merely documented policy. First, the discovering stage never adjudicates its own request — Build can open a Change/Reopening Request, but only the Design Boss can certify the resulting scope, exactly as no worker anywhere in this architecture certifies its own work (§0.4). Second, the certified Design baseline is unchanged until the request clears certification — Build does not get to work ahead against its own assumption of what the answer will be; it resumes only after Design Boss certification, so there is never a window where uncertified scope is being implemented as if it were certified. Third, every artifact created through this process carries the same ID, metadata, and evidence requirements as an original Discovery artifact (§0.12–0.13), including which pass or change request discovered it — so a reopened and later-certified requirement is indistinguishable, in the traceability chain (§T), from one Discovery found on the first pass. Nothing enters the Master Product Inventory through a side door. And when this process revises an artifact that was already certified rather than adding a wholly new one, the prior certified state is never overwritten — it is retained exactly as §T.4 specifies, so a reopened requirement leaves a permanent record of what was certified before and why it changed.

3.3 Test Boss — Determining Whether the Product Has Actually Been Tested
The Test Boss’s independent reconstruction produces an expected coverage matrix — every certified ACTION-, STATE-, VALIDATION-, PERMISSION-, SECURITY-, A11Y-, PERF-, and EDGECASE- artifact crossed against the test case(s) that should exercise it — derived without reading the Test Worker Corps’s actual test suite first. The diff against actual coverage identifies, by ID: artifacts with zero tests (a coverage gap regardless of how many tests exist elsewhere), artifacts with tests that execute but do not actually assert the certified behavior (a false-coverage gap — the most common failure mode counts alone hide), and artifacts over-tested with redundant cases that do not improve confidence (noise that makes the suite harder to maintain without making the product more correct). As with Design and Build, a larger test suite is never itself evidence of adequate testing — the Test Boss’s judgment is scoped to the diff between expected and actual coverage by artifact ID, not to the raw count of tests written or the percentage reported passing.

4.5 Operations Boss — Detecting Drift from the Certified Product
The Operations Boss’s distinguishing responsibility is drift detection: independently deriving what the live system’s configuration, monitoring coverage, and behavior should be from the certified OPS-/SECURITY- requirement sets and the current Living Blueprint, then comparing that expectation against what the Configuration & Drift Worker actually observes in production. A DRIFT-000n finding — a configuration value that no longer matches what was certified, a monitor that existed at deployment time but has since gone silent, a production behavior that no longer matches its certified -DEPLOY artifact — is never resolved by quietly updating the certified baseline to match what production happens to be doing. Drift is routed one of two ways: restored to match the certified state (the common case, when production has simply degraded from what it should be), or, if the live system’s actual need has genuinely outgrown what was certified, routed through the Change/Reopening Process (§2.4) so the new requirement is properly discovered, designed, and certified rather than silently absorbed as the new normal. This is the same fail-closed principle established in §2.3 and §2.4, applied to the live system rather than to Build: production drift is never itself evidence that the certified product was wrong.

5.2 Continuous Engineering Boss — Regression Protection
The Continuous Engineering Boss’s specific responsibility that has no equivalent in Stages 1–4: verifying that a change scoped to fix or extend one part of the certified product has not silently altered the certified behavior of any other part. A change that passes its own intended test cases but silently regresses an unrelated, previously CERTIFIED COMPLETE artifact is caught here, by ID, before it is allowed to merge — this is the mechanism, not merely the policy, behind “never break working software to fix broken software.”
§5.5 — Engineering Memory as the Platform’s Long-Term Ledger (Preserved, Extended)
Because every artifact — from a Pass-1 Discovery brief to a Stage-5 regression-protected fix applied three years into production — shares one ID system and one state machine, Engineering Memory is not a changelog bolted onto the application after the fact. It is the same Application Knowledge Graph, still growing, still queryable by ID, still subject to the same verification chain, now additionally recording every DEPLOYED-VERIFIED event (§4.1) and every Continuous Engineering certification (§5.1) as first-class history alongside the original Discovery-through-Test chain.

CROSS-CUTTING — SUPPORTING PLATFORM SYSTEMS
Continuous Learning Engine & AI Router (Preserved)
Both preserved exactly as originally specified. The Continuous Learning Engine studies what happened across the full lifecycle of every completed project — which Risk & Assumption Register entries proved correct, where the Multi-Perspective Reasoning Council caught or missed something, which Digital Twin elements required late revision, what the Operations Layer observed in production — and feeds these findings back into the Application Intelligence System, the Product DNA Library, and every specialist’s own reasoning patterns. In this edition, because every finding across all five stages is tied to an artifact ID and, ultimately, to the Discovery pass that first created that artifact’s lineage, the Continuous Learning Engine can attribute a lesson precisely across the entire lifecycle — not only “Pass 12, Edge-Case Discovery, under-specified payment-reversal states,” but equally “Stage 3, Permission Test Worker, under-tested the Manager/Admin permission boundary for delete actions three projects running,” feeding a correction back into both the Product DNA Library and the Test Worker Corps’s own standing coverage templates. The AI Router continues to select the best-suited model per task across providers, unchanged in mechanism, now logging which model produced or verified which artifact at every stage as part of that artifact’s metadata (§0.13), so model performance by task type and by stage becomes another input the Continuous Learning Engine can reason about.

§T — The Cross-Lifecycle Traceability Chain
Discovery is the foundation. Every stage after it exists to faithfully execute against the certified Master Product Inventory, never to reinterpret or silently narrow it. This section makes that chain of custody explicit and mechanically queryable end to end.
T.1 The Extended Artifact ID Chain
Every Discovery artifact ID (§0.12) is extended, one stage-specific suffix at a time, as it moves through the lifecycle — never replaced, always extended, so the full history remains in one ID lineage:
PAGE-0042                              (Discovery: certified, Pass 9)
  → PAGE-0042-DESIGN                   (Stage 1: certified, DESIGN-VERIFIED)
    → PAGE-0042-IMPL                   (Stage 2: certified, CODE-VERIFIED)
      → PAGE-0042-TEST                 (Stage 3: certified, TEST-VERIFIED)
        → PAGE-0042-DEPLOY             (Stage 4: certified, DEPLOYED-VERIFIED)
          → PAGE-0042-OPS              (Stage 5: continuously monitored, Living Blueprint node)
The same extension pattern applies to every artifact class — FEATURE-, ACTION-, STATE-, VALIDATION-, RULE-, PERMISSION-, API-, ENTITY-, INTEGRATION- — so that any artifact, at any point in the application’s life, has one unbroken ID lineage from the Discovery pass that first identified it through whatever is currently running in production.
T.2 Standing Queries the Platform Must Always Be Able to Answer
Because the ID lineage in T.1 is mandatory, not optional metadata, the platform can answer each of the following mechanically, by graph traversal, rather than by re-investigation:
“What Discovery artifact caused this design?” — traverse *-DESIGN backward to its root Discovery ID; the answer includes the discovery pass, worker, and evidence that originally certified it (§0.13).
“What Design artifact caused this implementation?” — traverse *-IMPL backward to *-DESIGN.
“What implementation is responsible for this requirement?” — traverse forward from a REQUIREMENT- or FEATURE- ID through every *-IMPL artifact whose lineage includes it.
“What test verifies it?” — traverse forward from any artifact ID to the *-TEST artifacts whose derived coverage (§3.1) cites it; an artifact with no forward edge into Stage 3 is, by construction, a coverage gap, not merely a hard-to-find one.
“What production component represents it?” — traverse forward through *-DEPLOY to the currently-live *-OPS node in the Living Blueprint.
T.3 Locating, Not Merely Detecting, a Gap
Because every query above is a graph traversal rather than a search, an incomplete chain is always locatable at the exact point it breaks: a PAGE-0042-DESIGN node with no forward -IMPL edge identifies precisely which page was designed but never built; a RULE-0091-IMPL node with no forward -TEST edge identifies precisely which business rule shipped untested; an -OPS node with no backward path to a Discovery ID identifies precisely which piece of running production code was never certified against anything — a structural anomaly the Continuous Engineering Boss (§5.2) treats as a Priority-1 finding regardless of how the code got there. The platform never reports “something is incomplete.” It reports the specific broken edge, by ID, at the specific stage transition where it broke.
T.4 Certification History Is Immutable
A previously certified baseline is never silently overwritten — by a Change/Reopening Request (§2.4), a production drift correction (§4.5), or a Stage 5 change (§5.1). When a certified artifact changes for any reason, the artifact’s ID does not move to a new certification; it accumulates one:
ORIGINAL CERTIFIED VERSION  (artifact ID, unchanged — see §T.1)
   ↓
CHANGE REQUEST  (reason for change, logged)
   ↓
IMPACT ANALYSIS  (Safe Change Intelligence — affected artifacts, logged)
   ↓
REVISED ARTIFACT  (same ID, new version)
   ↓
RE-VERIFICATION  (Self-Verification → Specialist Verifier, as at original certification)
   ↓
NEW CERTIFICATION  (by the same Boss role that would certify this artifact class,
   e.g. the Design Boss for a -DESIGN artifact, the Discovery Boss for a Discovery-level
   requirement)
The artifact’s change_history field (§0.13) is append-only: the previous certified state, the reason for change, the affected artifacts identified by the impact analysis, the impact analysis itself, the revised artifact, the verification evidence, and the new certification are all retained as a permanent sequence, never replaced or deleted. This requires no new versioning system beyond what §0.13’s metadata schema and §T.1’s ID lineage already carry — an artifact’s current certified state is simply the latest entry in a history that is never pruned. Engineering Memory (§5.5) and the Living Blueprint (§5) read directly from this same history: the Living Blueprint always reflects the latest certified state, while Engineering Memory retains every certified state that preceded it, so the platform can always answer not only what is currently true of an artifact but what was certified true of it at any earlier point, and why it changed.

§A — Adversarial Self-Review of the Architecture
Applying the same discipline the Discovery Red Team applies to a product inventory, this section attacks the architecture itself: assume it is still incomplete; find what a determined worker, a lazy Boss, or a stage boundary could still get away with. Each finding below is resolved with a specific strengthening already reflected in the sections above; they are restated together here as a single audit trail, because an architecture that claims rigor should be able to show its own adversarial review rather than only asserting the review was done.
Finding 1 — “What can still be skipped?” A Design, Build, Test, or Deployment worker could in principle produce output for the artifacts it finds easy and quietly omit the ones it finds hard, and a Boss that only checks totals would not notice specific omissions hiding inside a plausible-looking aggregate. Resolution: every Boss in this architecture (§1.3, §2.3, §3.3, §4.2, §5.2) is required to diff by artifact ID, never by count, and to enumerate missing artifacts individually rather than reporting a delta number — this was true of the Discovery Boss from the first edition and is now uniform across every stage.
Finding 2 — “What can still silently pass?” A worker could report “done” and, absent an engaged verifier, that report could stand unchallenged if the Specialist Verifier trusted the report’s narrative instead of inspecting the underlying evidence. Resolution: §0.18’s evidence-based verification standard — inspect what a count actually represents, never accept a self-reported tally at face value — is invoked explicitly in every stage’s verification chain (§1.2, §2.2, §3.2, §4.1, §5.1), and Test Boss discipline (§3.3) specifically calls out tests that execute without asserting certified behavior as a distinct, commonly-hidden failure mode.
Finding 3 — “Where can a worker claim completion without evidence?” The state machine (§0.17) could in principle be advanced by a worker’s own status update if no gate actually checked before writing the new state. Resolution: the architecture states explicitly, at §0.4 and again at §T.3, that no artifact’s state advances except through its stage’s governance gate — a worker’s declaration changes nothing about state, and an -OPS node with no certified backward lineage is treated as a structural anomaly precisely because it would represent this failure mode having occurred.
Finding 4 — “Where can a Boss fail to notice something?” Every Boss role in this architecture reconstructs its expectation from an upstream baseline — but a Boss that reconstructed carelessly, or that unconsciously anchored on the workers’ actual output despite the instruction not to, would produce a diff that rubber-stamps errors. Resolution: this is why Stage 5 adds a Continuous Engineering Boss check specifically for regressions the original change-maker did not think to test (§5.2), and why every stage’s Independent Audit role (Discovery Auditor DW-E5, Master Verification Engine as Stage 1’s audit, Build/Test/Deployment Auditors) exists as a second, structurally separate check on the Boss’s own judgment — the same separation of production from judgment applied a second time, so a single compromised or careless Boss cannot silently certify incomplete work.
Finding 5 — “Where can two stages disagree?” Discovery could certify one thing, Design could interpret it slightly differently, and both could independently believe they were correct — a disagreement neither stage’s own verification chain would surface, because each stage only checks itself against its own immediate upstream. Resolution: the Contradiction Engine (§0.19, DW-E2) is scoped in the original edition to Discovery-internal contradictions; this edition extends its charter implicitly through the T.2 traversal queries — a Design artifact whose behavior contradicts its cited Discovery artifact’s certified specification is now detectable as a specific, ID-addressable inconsistency during the Design Boss’s reconstruction (§1.3), not left to be discovered only when a later stage happens to notice the drift.
Finding 6 — “Where can an artifact disappear between stages?” The original two-stage Boss pattern (Discovery Boss, then a generically-described “Master Verification Engine” for everything after) left open exactly how a dropped artifact between, say, Build and Test would ever surface, since neither stage’s Boss was explicitly required to check the other’s inventory against a shared ID lineage. Resolution: §T.1’s mandatory ID-extension chain and §T.3’s traversal-based gap detection close this directly — a dropped artifact is not inferred from a mismatched count, it is the specific, named point where an expected forward edge in the ID lineage does not exist.
Finding 7 — “Where can counts look correct while the actual work is incomplete?” This is the failure mode the original request named most insistently, and it recurs at every stage: a Design pass that produces exactly 200 pages, all shallow; a Build pass that implements exactly the right number of endpoints, several with the wrong behavior; a Test pass with a green dashboard built on tests that do not assert what they claim to. Resolution: the eleven-dimension verification standard (§0.16) was written for Discovery in the first edition but is invoked by name in every later stage’s verification chain in this edition (§1.2, §2.2, §3.2, §4.1) specifically so that Count is never sufficient on its own anywhere in the pipeline — Identity, Correctness, Quality, and Evidence must each independently clear before any certification is issued, at every stage, not only the first one.
No finding above required inventing a new mechanism from scratch — each is resolved by extending the Worker → Self-Verification → Specialist Verifier → Boss → Independent Audit → Certification pattern and the artifact-ID/evidence discipline that already governed Discovery outward to the stages that previously described verification in softer, less mechanically specific terms. That consistency is itself the point: an architecture with one rigorously specified department and four aspirational ones is only as strong as its weakest stage, and the purpose of this revision is to make every stage as hard to fool as Discovery already was.
PART III — IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP, UPDATED FOR THE DISCOVERY ORGANIZATION
The original roadmap’s core property is preserved and remains the governing constraint on this edition: every phase is additive, nothing built early is reworked to reach the full vision, and each phase implements a subset of the same architecture rather than a different one. This edition adds one more increment to Level 1, because a Discovery Department with a Boss, a Red Team, and an eleven-dimension verification standard is a meaningfully heavier first release than a single-pass discovery engine — and because shipping it in two steps keeps the MVP genuinely minimal without abandoning the discipline that makes the platform Blueprint-First rather than prompt-to-code.

Two sequencing notes follow from making Discovery a real department rather than a single engine. First, the artifact ID and metadata schema (§0.12–0.13) must exist from Level 1b onward even in skeletal form, because retrofitting stable IDs onto an already-running Knowledge Graph is precisely the kind of structural rework the roadmap’s additive property is designed to avoid. Second, the Discovery Boss’s independent-reconstruction discipline (§0.15) can be introduced narrowly at Level 1b — reconstructing only page, feature, and workflow counts — and widened at Level 3 to cover the full Worker Corps, rather than waiting for the full department before any independent judgment exists in the pipeline at all.
A third note follows from extending the Worker → Self-Verification → Specialist Verifier → Boss → Independent Audit → Certification pattern through every later stage (§1–§5, §T, §A): the Design Boss, Build Boss, Test Boss, Deployment Boss, and Continuous Engineering Boss roles do not need to exist in full at Level 1a — the roadmap’s original lightweight AI Design Studio and AI Build Studio remain the correct MVP scope — but the -DESIGN/-IMPL/-TEST/-DEPLOY/-OPS ID-extension convention (§T.1) should be adopted from Level 1a onward for the same reason artifact IDs are adopted early in Discovery: retrofitting a traceability lineage onto artifacts that were never ID-bound in the first place is exactly the structural rework the roadmap exists to avoid. Each stage’s Boss role is then introduced at the Level where that stage’s own department first reaches full specification — the Design Boss at Level 2 alongside the Master Verification Engine, the Build and Test Bosses at Level 3 alongside the full Discovery Department they depend on, and the Deployment and Continuous Engineering Bosses at Levels 4 and 5 respectively, alongside the systems they govern.

PART IV — THE LONG-TERM VISION & THE CATEGORY (PRESERVED)
The category this platform defines — Blueprint-First AI Software Engineering — is unchanged by this edition, and so is the argument for it: this is not another AI coding assistant, IDE, or website builder, but a refusal of the assumption that AI-built software must be written before it is understood, in the tradition of Kubernetes, Git, Docker, Stripe, Figma, and AWS each naming an assumption the rest of the industry had stopped questioning.
What this edition adds to that argument is a sharper claim about why the refusal holds up under real engineering pressure rather than only in a pitch. A platform that merely asserts “discovery before design” is a philosophy. A platform whose Discovery layer is a real department — with named workers, independent verifiers, a Boss that reconstructs rather than reviews, a Red Team paid only for genuine omissions, and a completeness standard that a count alone cannot satisfy — is an engineering organization that can be audited, the same way a serious company’s engineering organization can be audited. That is the difference this edition is built to make structural: not a stronger claim about completeness, but a mechanism that makes an incomplete or unverified claim mechanically difficult to make in the first place.
Above all of this still sits the AI Executive Orchestrator and, beneath it now, a Chief Discovery Officer with genuine independent authority — ensuring that before a single design decision is made, the organization already knows, completely and provably, what it is building. Discovery before design, design before code, verification at every step: this is what allows the platform to act not as a tool that generates software, but as an organization that engineers it, and — through the Permanent Engineering Organization — one that never stops owning what it built.

Appendix C: Document Provenance & Editorial Notes
This document has now passed through two rounds of enhancement beyond the original manifesto, and both are preserved intact rather than collapsed into one another.
Round one turned Stage 0 from a set of engines into a real Discovery Department: the Worker→Self-Verification→Specialist Verifier→Boss→Independent Audit→Certification model, the 25-pass Discovery sequence, the 14-layer Recursive Page Expansion, the Industry Comparison Engine, the Discovery Red Team, Negative-Space Discovery, the Master Product Inventory with its artifact ID and metadata schema, the eleven-dimension verification standard, and the Definition-of-Complete state machine. Round one’s Discovery Worker Corps was originally presented as five cluster tables plus one fully-specified representative role per cluster.
Round two, reflected in this version, does two things and nothing else. First, it replaces round one’s table-plus-representative treatment of the Discovery Worker Corps with all 36 roles individually specified in full (§0.3) across twelve fields each — responsibility, inputs, outputs/artifacts, evidence required, dependencies, verification, failure conditions, rejection/rework behavior, interaction with the Discovery Boss, interaction with the Red Team/Auditor, contribution to recursive discovery, and contribution to the Master Product Inventory — because Discovery is the foundation everything downstream must faithfully execute against, and a foundation described only by representative example is not yet a foundation described completely. Second, it extends round one’s governance discipline, previously stated only as a general principle for Stages 1 through 5 (“what changes”), into the same fully worked structure Discovery already had: named worker corps, named Boss roles (Design Boss, Build Boss, Test Boss, Deployment Boss, Continuous Engineering Boss), named Independent Audit roles, and — new in round two — an explicit end-to-end artifact-ID lineage (§T) and a named adversarial self-review of the architecture itself (§A), because a pipeline is only as disciplined as its least-specified stage, and round one left Stages 1 through 5 as intent rather than mechanism.
Nothing from the original manifesto or from round one is removed, renamed, or weakened in round two. Every addition either extends an existing mechanism outward to a stage that did not yet have it, or names explicitly a discipline the earlier rounds already asserted in principle. The 25 Discovery passes, the 14 expansion layers, the Industry Comparison Engine, the Red Team, Negative-Space Discovery, the Master Product Inventory, stable artifact IDs, the eleven-dimension standard, the Definition-of-Complete state machine, traceability, certification, the Digital Twin, and the original roadmap, architecture map, and glossary all remain exactly as round one specified them, now joined by their counterparts in Stages 1 through 5.
Round three, reflected in this version, is intentionally the narrowest of the three: three targeted corrections to an architecture that round two had already brought to full specification, plus the consistency check those corrections require — not a further expansion of scope. First, Boss independence (§0.15) is strengthened so that every Boss’s expectation is built only from the original Product Understanding Brief and the platform’s trusted foundational knowledge, never from any worker-produced conclusion — closing the risk that a Boss could unknowingly inherit and re-confirm an early worker’s mistake — and the same rule is stated explicitly as governing every later-stage Boss (§1.3, §2.3, §3.3, §4.2, §4.5, §5.2). Second, the Build Boss’s prior ability to retroactively certify Build-discovered scope is removed; a coding worker’s finding that something is missing is now, without exception, either proven to already fall under an existing certified artifact or routed through the new Change/Reopening Process (§2.4), which is fail-closed by construction — the discovering stage never adjudicates its own request, and the certified baseline never moves until the Design Boss certifies the addition on its own merits. Third, Stage 4 is expanded from a deployment-verification chain into a complete, continuously-operating Operations Department (§4.3–§4.5): seven sensible worker roles, an Operations Boss distinct from the Deployment Boss, an Operations Auditor, and explicit drift detection against the Living Blueprint, governed by the same Worker→Self-Verification→Specialist Verifier→Boss→Independent Audit→Certification chain used everywhere else in the architecture.
Nothing from round one or round two is removed, renamed, or weakened. All 36 Discovery workers, the 25 Discovery passes, Recursive Page Expansion’s 14 layers, the Industry Comparison Engine, Negative-Space Discovery, the Discovery Red Team and Auditor, the Master Product Inventory, stable artifact IDs and metadata, the eleven-dimension verification standard, evidence-based verification, the Definition-of-Complete state machine, the Contradiction Engine, the Risk & Assumption Register, the Multi-Perspective Reasoning Council, both traceability engines, the Product Coverage Engine, Blueprint Completeness Certification and Confidence, the Design/Build/Test Worker Corps and Bosses, Deployment verification and the Deployment Boss, the Continuous Engineering Boss, cross-lifecycle traceability, the adversarial self-review, and the roadmap, glossary, architecture map, and this provenance appendix all remain exactly as round two specified them — now with the three corrections above applied consistently across every stage that touches them, and with this architecture, as of round three, treated as the finalized target: the next step is implementation-readiness and implementation itself, not a further architectural expansion.
